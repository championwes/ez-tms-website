LTL vs. Truckload: When to Use Each

Mode selection is one of the quietest levers on your margin. Get it right and the load practically prices itself; get it wrong and you’ve either overpaid for empty trailer space or crammed a full truck’s worth of freight onto a network that wasn’t built for it. Here’s a practical way to think about the choice.

The short version

As a rule of thumb, freight that fills fewer than six pallets or weighs under roughly 15,000 pounds is a candidate for LTL. Beyond that — or when the freight is fragile, time-critical, or high-value — full truckload usually wins.

When LTL makes sense

  • Smaller shipments: one to six pallets that don’t justify a whole trailer.
  • Flexible timing: LTL networks consolidate freight, so transit is less predictable — fine when the delivery window is generous.
  • Cost sensitivity: the shipper only pays for the space they use.

When truckload wins

  • Volume: enough freight to fill — or nearly fill — a trailer.
  • Fragility: fewer touches means less handling damage, since the freight isn’t transferred between terminals.
  • Speed: a dedicated truck goes point to point without consolidation stops.
The best brokers don’t guess at mode — they rate both in seconds and let the numbers decide.

That last point is where the right software earns its keep. Instead of quoting one mode and hoping, you should be able to rate LTL and truckload side by side on the same screen, compare against live capacity, and give the customer a confident answer while they’re still on the phone.
